# Research Question

## Primary question

Can baseline radiographic, patient-reported, clinical, and physical-performance information
predict future OA progression better than any individual measurement domain alone?

## Intended comparison

The future study will compare each eligible single-domain approach with a linked multimodal
late-fusion approach, using the same eligible participants/knees, outcome definition, prediction
horizon, and evaluation framework wherever scientifically appropriate.

## Population, index time, and outcome

These elements are intentionally unresolved:

- the eligible OAI population and knee-level inclusion/exclusion rules;
- the OAI visit that can validly serve as baseline for all required domains;
- the follow-up horizon or horizons;
- radiographic, symptomatic, or composite progression criteria;
- treatment or surgery-related censoring/exclusion rules; and
- whether physical-performance measures are sufficiently complete and temporally aligned.

They will be defined only after the real release structure, documentation, and longitudinal
availability are reviewed. No OAI variable names or visit codes are assumed here.

## Unit of analysis and dependence

The scientific outcome will be evaluated at the knee level if supported by the actual linked
data. Knees are nested within participants. Any future train/validation/test partition must keep
all knees from one participant together. No partitioning is implemented in this milestone.

## Evidence needed before preregistration or analysis

- official OAI release documentation and data-use constraints;
- observed linkage coverage across the proposed domains;
- visit-level availability and timing;
- missingness by domain, visit, participant, and knee where applicable;
- image availability and quality-control results; and
- candidate outcome prevalence and follow-up completeness under reviewed definitions.

All substantive choices and changes will be recorded in the learning journal and the relevant
protocol document.
